werx

A private AI assistant that reads your own calendar and email, so you can ask questions about them in plain language.

This is a personal tool, not a service. It has one user — its owner. There is no sign-up, no public access, and no third party who can reach your data.

What it does

werx connects to your Google account using Google's official OAuth sign-in. Once authorised, you can ask things like:

  • “What's on my calendar this week?”
  • “Do I have anything to do today?”
  • “Summarise the mail I haven't read.”
  • “When is my next appointment that needs travel time?”

The assistant answers using your real calendar and inbox, rather than relying on memory or on you re-typing what you already keep in Google.

Why it needs access, and how little it uses

Reading your calendar and inbox requires two permissions from Google. It asks for nothing else:

PermissionWhy it is needed
calendarRead events so it can answer questions about your schedule
gmail.readonlySearch and read messages so it can find mail you haven't dealt with

It does not request access to Contacts, Drive, Docs, Sheets, or any other Google service. It cannot send email on your behalf, delete messages, or modify anything in your account. It is read-only by design.

How your data is handled

  • Calendar and email data is processed in memory on the owner's own computer and then discarded.
  • Nothing is written to a database, sold, shared, or used for advertising.
  • No data is used to train any machine learning model.
  • An OAuth token is stored locally so the tool does not ask you to log in every time. It is protected by normal file permissions and is only ever sent to Google's own authentication servers.

Where it runs

The assistant runs on a personal home server, and is reachable only by its owner. Only the Google account connection itself relies on Google's servers, which is how any Google sign-in works.
